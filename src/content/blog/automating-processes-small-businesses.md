For years, process automation was associated with large corporations with dedicated IT teams. Today, tools like n8n or Make let you connect applications without writing thousands of lines of code, putting automation within reach of small and mid-sized businesses.

## Start with what's repetitive, not what's complex

The most common mistake is trying to automate the most complex process in the business on day one. It's better to start with simple, repetitive tasks: sending a confirmation email, logging a lead in a spreadsheet, notifying the team when a new order arrives.

## Measure before and after

Before automating any process, it helps to measure how long it takes to do manually. That number is what justifies the investment and, later on, shows the real impact of automation.

## Automation doesn't replace strategy

Automating a poorly designed process just makes the mistakes happen faster. Before automating, it's worth checking whether the process itself actually makes sense.
